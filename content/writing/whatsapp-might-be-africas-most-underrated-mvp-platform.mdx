When founders talk about a minimum viable product, they usually mean a smaller app. A simpler website. A prototype with fewer screens.

But for a lot of ideas, the most useful first version isn't software at all. It's a WhatsApp group.

## Where people already are

WhatsApp is already on the phone. People already open it many times a day. They already know how to use it. There is nothing to download, no account to create, and nothing new to learn.

That matters more than it sounds. Every new step you add to a product is a place where someone can quietly drop off — and when you are trying to learn whether an idea works, you don't want to confuse "people didn't want this" with "people didn't install this."

## What a group can test

[Fitness Space](/work/fitness-space) began this way. Before we invested heavily in software, we ran the system through WhatsApp groups with real people trying to lose weight and live healthier.

A group let us test the parts that mattered: whether the plan made sense, whether check-ins helped, whether accountability kept people going, and where it all broke down. We could change the approach from one day to the next without a release cycle.

## What it can't do

WhatsApp doesn't scale on its own. Some things are hard to measure in a chat. Personalisation gets harder as groups grow.

That is the point where technology earns its place — not before. When we finally built software, we were building for problems we had already watched happen.

## Start there

If you are building for people who already live on WhatsApp, try starting there. It is cheap, it is fast, and it puts you close enough to your users to see what they actually do.
